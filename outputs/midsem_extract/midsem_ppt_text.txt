Speech:

Psychomotor:
Agitation or retardation in physical movement

Limitations of Existing Methods
PDPM IIITDM Jabalpur | Depression Detection using TSFFM
Single Modality Weakness
Relying solely on face OR body leads to incomplete depression assessment. Each modality captures only part of the clinical picture.
Subjectivity in Labeling

Generalizability Issues

Proposed Solution: Two-Stream Feature Fusion Model
PDPM IIITDM Jabalpur | Depression Detection using TSFFM
The TSFFM simultaneously processes two types of visual information from the same video — facial expressions and body posture — through separate CNN streams, then fuses them to make a holistic depression prediction.
Stream 1: Facial
Detects and crops face region
CNN extracts expression features
Encodes emotion: sadness, flatness, gaze
Stream 2: Body
MediaPipe pose estimation
Skeleton joint coordinates
Encodes posture: slump, energy, movement

--- Slide 11 ---
Data Preprocessing
PDPM IIITDM Jabalpur | Depression Detection using TSFFM
Step 1
Frame Extraction
Input video loaded using OpenCV (cv2.VideoCapture)
Frames extracted at uniform intervals (e.g., every 5th frame)
Reduces redundancy while retaining temporal diversity
Extracted frames stored as NumPy arrays for processing
Step 2
Face Detection
OpenCV Haar Cascade or DNN-based face detector applied
Bounding box coordinates extracted for face region
Face region cropped and resized to 224×224 pixels
Normalized pixel values to [0,1] range for CNN input
Step 3
Pose Estimation
MediaPipe Holistic model applied to each frame
Extracts 33 body landmark coordinates (x, y, z, visibility)
Landmarks flattened into a 132-dimensional feature vector
Handles occlusion and missing keypoints gracefully

--- Slide 19 ---
Challenges Encountered
PDPM IIITDM Jabalpur | Depression Detection using TSFFM
01
Dataset Limitations
Depression datasets are scarce and ethically constrained. Most publicly available datasets (DAIC-WOZ, AVEC) are small and have strict access controls. This limits model generalizability and increases overfitting risk.
02
Computational Constraints
Training deep CNNs on CPU is slow. Limited GPU access meant fewer training epochs (5) were feasible. This reduces the model's convergence and confidence in predictions.
03
Synchronization Complexity
Aligning the facial and body streams temporally is challenging. Dropped frames, detection failures, or frame skipping can introduce mismatches between the two streams.
04
Occlusion & Detection Failures
Faces or body parts may be partially visible or out of frame. OpenCV face detection and MediaPipe can fail under poor lighting, extreme angles, or fast movement.
